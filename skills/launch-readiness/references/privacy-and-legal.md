# Privacy facts and legal applicability

Prepare a privacy draft from the actual operator, product, audience, markets and data flows. The draft must describe collection, purposes, recipients/providers, retention, deletion and user contact accurately. Store-disclosure definitions differ; inspect the official definitions before filling forms.

For Google Play, verify a publicly accessible policy URL and in-app access. Check that the operator/app identity and disclosed practices match the listing. The policy also needs retention/deletion information. Do not rely on a PDF, geo-restricted page or local draft for that public-policy gate.

For account-based apps, test real deletion rather than account freezing. Inventory associated backend records, processors, logs, model memory and backups. If legitimate retention applies, record its reason and periods accurately; do not invent a zero-retention guarantee. Android needs the applicable in-app and web request paths.

For Apple, reconcile privacy declarations with integrated partners and actual collection. Review manifests/SDK requirements from current documentation. A manifest is not a replacement for accurate public disclosures or runtime consent.

Keep factual confirmation separate from legal judgment. Record target jurisdictions, controller/operator, audience, sensitive/regulated uses, international providers/transfers, content/IP licenses and applicable contract/consumer obligations. Determine which laws apply from authoritative sources and qualified advice where needed. Do not assume GDPR, children's rules or regulated-sector obligations are either universal or irrelevant.

A generated policy cannot create evidence of consent, lawful handling, a valid vendor agreement or a tested deletion process. Only include commitments the system/operator can honor. Unresolved applicability, missing business facts, ambiguous data flows or required legal review produces review needed, not an invented approval.

An app's future legal exposure depends on ongoing conduct, policy changes and jurisdiction. The checkpoint reduces identifiable launch omissions; it cannot promise immunity from legal trouble.
